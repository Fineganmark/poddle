Christ Church Cathedral

Christ Church stands on the ridge above the Poddle and the Liffey, at the end of Lord Edward Street. It was founded around 1028, after the Norse king Sitric Silkenbeard went on pilgrimage to Rome, with Dúnán as its first bishop. Henry II heard Mass here at Christmas 1171. It was rebuilt in stone from the 1180s, and its nave in the 1230s. In 1487 a pretender, Lambert Simnel, was crowned here, and in 1551 the service was sung in English for the first time in Ireland. George Edmund Street rebuilt much of it between 1871 and 1878, paid for by the distiller Henry Roe, and joined it by his covered bridge to the new Synod Hall, which takes in the tower of the old church of St Michael. The road schemes and the loss of the old houses at Wood Quay left it standing on its own.

St Patrick’s, down by the river, often flooded, and in 1744 its chapter had to ask to hold their Lenten services at Christ Church, because their own cathedral was “dangerous to assemble in from the late floods”. Christ Church had its own water trouble in April 1953, when water was seeping into the basement of the Synod Hall. A water diviner found water 21 feet under High Street, though workmen digging 18 feet under the hall found none. Some blamed the Poddle; others a spring that had come back to life.
