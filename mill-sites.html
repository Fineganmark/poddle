The mills of the Poddle, one by one

Nine mill sites, some of them groups of neighbours, from Templeogue to New Street, in order from source to sea. Select a mill marker on the map to land on its entry here.

Templeogue No map marker

Mount Down Mill (Domville Mill)

Duncan’s 1821 map of Dublin shows Domville Mill at Templeogue, upstream of Kimmage. Old accounts of the City Watercourse say the Poddle joined it at Mount Down Flour Mills. The 1906 to 1909 Ordnance Survey map labels it Mount Down Mill and shows it as disused. Local histories use the mill to mark the end of the Tymon River section.

Kimmage Road Lower and Kimmage Cross Roads Farrington’s site approximate

Ravensdale Mills and Farrington’s Mill

Two mills worked side by side on this stretch. Ravensdale Mills was a corn and flour mill with its own pond, sluice and weir, on maps from the 1830s until 1940. Farrington’s Mill, beside Kimmage Cross Roads, was a flour mill that worked by water turbine until the 1920s. The 1843 Ordnance Survey map also shows Cutlers Mill and its millrace, and a flour mill with a pond in the Kimmage demesne, and the flood scheme’s heritage report says remains of the Ravensdale mill and Cutlers millrace may survive below ground. Ravensdale Park, beside the site, is one of the places being reshaped to store flood water in the flood alleviation scheme.

Sundrive Shopping Centre and St Martin’s Drive Approximate site

Larkfield Mills and the St Martin’s Drive mill

Larkfield Mills, a mill with its pond, stood where the Sundrive Shopping Centre is now, and the name Larkfield survives in the area. Close by, near St Martin’s Drive, the first Ordnance Survey map shows another mill, a pond and a windmill together. The 1876 map names Tinker Mill and Larkfield Mill in the Brook Lawn and Sundrive Road area.

Kimmage Road Lower Approximate site

Loader’s Park Mills

Local histories describe a corn mill on Kimmage Road Lower, and the building survives as the Waterwheel apartments. The flood scheme’s heritage report lists Loader’s Park Mill as a paper mill on the 1906 to 1909 map, south-east of Mount Argus, so the site may have changed use over time. The report finds no significant impact on its millrace.

Mount Argus Road

Mount Argus mills

In the 19th century there was a paper mill here, and a flour mill on Mount Argus Road that later became a laundry. The Stone Boat, where the river divides, is nearby.

Greenmount and Our Lady’s Hospice, Harold’s Cross

Greenmount Mills and the Hospice mill pond

The National Monuments Service records a mill site here. Greenmount was a corn mill that became a cotton mill by 1807 and was enlarged by the Quaker Pim family for cotton and linen. Beside it, a mill pond by Our Lady’s Hospice is shown on the 1910 Ordnance Survey map, and Rev. Myles Ronan’s 1927 map shows a mill pond and a Wood Mill on this part of the river, with the dotted line of an older water course beside them. Greenmount’s mill pond was among the last to disappear, covered over in the late 1960s. The lanes around it now hold small businesses.

Mill Street and Sweeney’s Lane Approximate site

Double mill, Mill Street

An oil and flour mill, linked to St Thomas’s Abbey, which gave Mill Street its name. Mill Street is first mentioned in 1711. By 1830 the street had several tanners and a fancy weaver, and by 1880 five tanners and a parchment maker.

New Street, beside St Nicholas’ Bridge

Medieval mill, Cross Poddle

A medieval mill beside St Nicholas’ Bridge, outside St Nicholas’ Gate, at the junction of New Street, Patrick Street and Kevin Street. The old Cross Poddle ran beside it.
