River levels, flood maps and models

What the water is doing now, and where to explore the flood models behind the scheme.

Is there a gauge on the Poddle?

Not one that publishes live readings. The Office of Public Works runs Ireland’s network of river gauges, and none of its public stations is on the Poddle. The nearest are on the Dodder, the river the monks borrowed water from at Balrothery, and the one the Lakelands Overflow still sends flood water back to. When the Dodder is high after heavy rain on the Dublin Mountains, the Poddle is usually rising too.

Explore the flood models

The flood scheme was designed with a computer model of the river: a hydraulic model that works out how deep and how fast the water runs in floods of different sizes. The model itself isn’t public, but its results are.

  • floodinfo.ie: the national flood map portal. Search for Harold’s Cross or Kimmage to see the modelled flood extents along the Poddle.
  • CFRAM river flood extents: the same flood outlines as open data you can download and map.
  • CFRAM model nodes: points along the modelled rivers with predicted levels and flows for each flood size, with future climate scenarios alongside.
  • CFRAM modelled river centrelines: the river lines the model uses.
  • River water quality status, 2016 to 2021: the EPA’s ecological status for every river, the Poddle included.
  • waterlevel.ie: all the Office of Public Works’ live gauges, with downloads.

A 1-in-100-year flood is a flood with a 1% chance of happening in any one year. It is the standard the Poddle scheme is built to.
