The river walk

Source to sea, stop by stop

The Poddle’s course has been created and recreated by nature and by people over the centuries. Here we plot it as a single line, above and below ground, as best we can. The map shows the same stops on today’s streets, and what the planning files say adds some of the most interesting facts.

  1. 01Fettercairn HouseTallaghtWhere the catchment begins, in the townland of Whitehall.
  2. 02BancroftTallaghtThe first stretch of the river you can walk beside.
  3. 03TymonTymon ParkA ruined castle, a glacial esker and the park built around them.
  4. 04Balrothery WeirFirhouse, on the DodderWhere medieval engineers turned the Dodder towards the city.Dodder water joins here
  5. 05M50 CrossingTymon ParkWhere the motorway sent the river underground.
  6. 06Dodder OverflowLakelands, TerenureA sluice that sends flood water back to the Dodder.
  7. 07Kimmage ManorKimmageA Victorian MP’s house, now a mission centre, built over the stream.
  8. 08Ravensdale and Poddle ParkKimmage Cross RoadsA valley of mills, and the scheme’s main flood storage in the city.
  9. 09The Stone BoatSundrive Road, KimmageThe medieval stone that splits the river two to one.⅓ of the flow leaves for St Thomas’s Abbey
  10. 10Mount ArgusHarold’s CrossFlood defences in a park, beside the church of a saint.
  11. 11Mount JeromeHarold’s CrossA Victorian cemetery, and perhaps another monks’ diversion.
